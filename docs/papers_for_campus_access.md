# Paywalled Papers: Campus Access Checklist

This document lists papers cited in the competency trap literature review that are likely to be paywalled or have limited open access. Use this as a checklist when accessing journals through your institution's library subscription.

---

## CORE COMPETENCY TRAP LITERATURE

### Foundational Organizational Theory

**1. March, J. G. (1991). Exploration and exploitation in organizational learning.**
- Journal: *Organization Science, 2*(1), 71-87.
- DOI: 10.1287/orsc.2.1.71
- Publisher: INFORMS
- Why important: Foundational theory establishing the exploration-exploitation tradeoff and the self-reinforcing nature of competency traps.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - March's work is classic organizational science, almost certainly behind INFORMS paywall.

**2. Levitt, B., & March, J. G. (1988). Organizational learning.**
- Journal: *Annual Review of Sociology, 14*, 319-340.
- DOI: 10.1146/annurev.so.14.080188.001535
- Publisher: Annual Reviews, Inc.
- Why important: Major review synthesizing behavioral theory of the firm with explicit definition of competency trap; distinguishes superstitious learning.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - Annual Review journals are typically paywalled.

**3. Levinthal, D. A., & March, J. G. (1993). The myopia of learning.**
- Journal: *Strategic Management Journal, 14*(S2), 95-112.
- DOI: 10.1002/smj.4250141009
- Publisher: Wiley
- Why important: Detailed analysis of three forms of myopia (temporal, spatial, failure) as structural features of learning systems.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - Wiley's Strategic Management Journal behind paywall. Check for author's working paper version on Stanford or University of Minnesota websites (where Levinthal was affiliated).

**4. Henderson, R. M., & Clark, K. B. (1990). Architectural innovation: The reconfiguration of existing product technologies and the failure of established firms.**
- Journal: *Administrative Science Quarterly, 35*(1), 9-30.
- DOI: 10.2307/2393549
- Publisher: JSTOR (Cornell University Press)
- Why important: Distinguishes component knowledge from architectural knowledge; explains why established firms fail at architectural innovations. Foundational for understanding technology-based competency traps.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Classic ASQ paper behind JSTOR. Check for working paper versions at MIT Sloan (Henderson's affiliation) or access through institutional JSTOR subscription.

**5. Tushman, M. L., & Anderson, P. (1986). Technological discontinuities and organizational environments.**
- Journal: *Administrative Science Quarterly, 31*(3), 439-465.
- DOI: 10.2307/2392832
- Publisher: JSTOR (Cornell University Press)
- Why important: Distinguishes competence-enhancing from competence-destroying discontinuities; quantitative evidence that competency trap activation depends on type of environmental change.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - ASQ behind JSTOR. Check Stanford or Columbia (Tushman's affiliations) repositories for working papers.

**6. Leonard-Barton, D. (1992). Core capabilities and core rigidities: A paradox in managing new product development.**
- Journal: *Strategic Management Journal, 13*(S1), 111-125.
- DOI: 10.1002/smj.4250131009
- Publisher: Wiley
- Why important: Multi-case evidence (20 NPD projects) that core capabilities have a paradoxical flip side. Values dimension most resistant to change.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - Wiley. Check Harvard Business School (Leonard-Barton's affiliation) or SSRN for working papers.

**7. Tripsas, M., & Gavetti, G. (2000). Capabilities, cognition, and inertia: Evidence from digital imaging.**
- Journal: *Strategic Management Journal, 21*(10-11), 1147-1161.
- DOI: 10.1002/1097-0266(200010/11)21:10/11<1147::AID-SMJ128>3.0.CO;2-R
- Publisher: Wiley
- Why important: Case study of Polaroid's failure in digital imaging; distinguishes capability inertia from cognitive inertia; mechanism-focused.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Wiley. Check Harvard Business School repository or Google Scholar for PDF link (authors often post).

**8. Audia, P. G., Locke, E. A., & Smith, K. G. (2000). The paradox of success: An archival and a laboratory study of strategic persistence following radical environmental change.**
- Journal: *Academy of Management Journal, 43*(5), 837-853.
- DOI: 10.5465/1556411
- Publisher: Academy of Management
- Why important: CRITICAL - Only prior controlled lab experiment directly testing competency trap mechanism; combines archival and experimental data.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - Academy of Management Journal. Check authors' institutional repositories; this is important enough to obtain.

**9. Sull, D. N. (1999). The dynamics of standing firm.**
- Journal: *Harvard Business Review, 77*(1), 56-70.
- Publisher: Harvard Business Publishing
- Why important: Introduces "active inertia" concept; distinguishes from passive inertia; four mechanisms (strategic frames, processes, relationships, values).
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - Harvard Business Review behind paywall. Check HBR.org or Harvard repository.

**10. Miller, D. (1994). What happens after success: The perils of excellence.**
- Journal: *Journal of Management Studies, 31*(3), 325-358.
- DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-6486.1994.tb00914.x
- Publisher: Wiley
- Why important: Develops "Icarus Paradox" concept; shows how success breeds failure; identifies four trajectories from success to failure.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - Wiley. Check for working papers at McGill (Miller's affiliation).

**11. Sorensen, J. B., & Stuart, T. E. (2000). Aging, obsolescence, and organizational innovation.**
- Journal: *Administrative Science Quarterly, 45*(1), 81-112.
- DOI: 10.2307/2667187
- Publisher: JSTOR (Cornell University Press)
- Why important: Quantitative study (patent data) showing older organizations innovate closer to existing technology; competency trap as developmental phenomenon.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - ASQ behind JSTOR. Sorensen or Stuart may have posted working papers at Stanford.

**12. Hannan, M. T., & Freeman, J. H. (1984). Structural inertia and organizational change.**
- Journal: *American Sociological Review, 49*(2), 149-164.
- DOI: 10.2307/2095567
- Publisher: JSTOR (American Sociological Association)
- Why important: Population ecology perspective; argues structural inertia is selected FOR by environment (major alternative explanation).
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - ASR behind JSTOR. Check for working papers at Stanford or UC Berkeley (authors' affiliations).

**13. Teece, D. J., Pisano, G., & Shuen, A. (1997). Dynamic capabilities and strategic management.**
- Journal: *Strategic Management Journal, 18*(7), 509-533.
- DOI: 10.1002/(SICI)1097-0266(199708)18:7<509::AID-SMJ882>3.0.CO;2-Z
- Publisher: Wiley
- Why important: Introduces "dynamic capabilities" framework; proposes these as answer to competency traps; emphasizes path dependence.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - Wiley. Check UC Berkeley (Teece's affiliation) for working papers or Google Scholar.

**14. Crossan, M. M., Lane, H. W., & White, R. E. (1999). An organizational learning framework: From intuition to institution.**
- Journal: *Academy of Management Review, 24*(3), 522-537.
- DOI: 10.5465/amr.1999.2202135
- Publisher: Academy of Management
- Why important: 4I framework showing how institutionalization can constrain individual learning; structural basis of competency traps.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - Academy of Management Review. Check institutional repository.

**15. Kelly, E., & Amburgey, T. L. (1991). Organizational inertia and momentum: A dynamic model of strategic change.**
- Journal: *Academy of Management Journal, 34*(3), 591-612.
- DOI: 10.5465/256406
- Publisher: Academy of Management
- Why important: Longitudinal quantitative study; distinguishes inertia from momentum; shows how past changes affect future change likelihood.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - Academy of Management Journal.

## COGNITIVE MECHANISMS

**16. Luchins, A. S. (1942). Mechanization in problem solving: The effect of Einstellung.**
- Journal: *Psychological Monographs, 54*(6), 1-43.
- DOI: 10.1037/h0093502
- Publisher: American Psychological Association
- Why important: CLASSIC - Water-jar experiments establishing mechanization of thought; stronger with more practice. Foundation for all cognitive entrenchment work.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - APA paywalled but very old (1942). Check Google Scholar or Internet Archive. May be freely available given age. APA often makes older papers available.

**17. Bilalic, M., McLeod, P., & Gobet, F. (2008a). Why good thoughts block better ones: The mechanism of the Einstellung (set) effect.**
- Journal: *Cognition, 108*(3), 652-661.
- DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2008.05.008
- Publisher: Elsevier
- Why important: Eye-tracking study with chess experts; shows attentional capture as mechanism of Einstellung; directly relevant to attention-based competency trap mechanisms.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Elsevier. Check University of Goettingen or Brunel University (authors' affiliations) for preprints.

**18. Bilalic, M., McLeod, P., & Gobet, F. (2008b). Inflexibility of experts—Reality or myth?**
- Journal: *Cognitive Psychology, 56*(2), 73-102.
- DOI: 10.1016/j.cogpsych.2007.04.001
- Publisher: Elsevier
- Why important: CRITICAL - Quantifies expert inflexibility; higher expertise = greater Einstellung in certain structures; expertise directly causes inflexibility.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Elsevier. Check Goettingen or Brunel for preprints.

**19. Bilalic, M., McLeod, P., & Gobet, F. (2010). The mechanism of the Einstellung (set) effect.**
- Journal: *Current Directions in Psychological Science, 19*(2), 111-115.
- DOI: 10.1177/0963721410363571
- Publisher: SAGE
- Why important: Review and theoretical synthesis of attentional mechanisms in Einstellung; connects to broader attention science.
- Open access status: CHECK SAGE - Some Current Directions articles are open access. SAGE has mixed OA policy. Try Google Scholar first.

**20. Dane, E. (2010). Reconsidering the trade-off between expertise and flexibility: A cognitive entrenchment perspective.**
- Journal: *Academy of Management Review, 35*(4), 579-603.
- DOI: 10.5465/amr.2010.53503239
- Publisher: Academy of Management
- Why important: Proposes cognitive entrenchment as distinct construct; distinguishes from rigidity; predicts expertise reduces flexibility through schema mechanisms.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Academy of Management Review. Check Rice University (Dane's affiliation) for working papers.

**21. Wiley, J. (1998). Expertise as mental set: The effects of domain knowledge in creative problem solving.**
- Journal: *Memory & Cognition, 26*(4), 716-730.
- DOI: 10.3758/BF03211392
- Publisher: Springer (Psychonomic Society)
- Why important: Shows domain knowledge impairs insight when it biases problem representation; expertise blocks alternative representations.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Springer. Psychonomic Society papers often have author PDFs available on ResearchGate.

**22. Bartlett, F. C. (1932). Remembering: A study in experimental and social psychology.**
- Publisher: Cambridge University Press
- Why important: CLASSIC FOUNDATIONAL - Introduces schemas; shows memory as reconstructive; basis for all schema-based competency trap explanations.
- Open access status: LIKELY OPEN - Published 1932; very old and foundational. Check Project MUSE, Google Books, or Internet Archive. Cambridge may have made available.

**23. Rumelhart, D. E., & Norman, D. A. (1978). Accretion, tuning, and restructuring: Three modes of learning.**
- In: G. A. Catania & A. M. Honig (Eds.), *Handbook of operant behavior*
- Publisher: Prentice-Hall
- Why important: Proposes three learning modes; restructuring hardest; explains why tuning is preferred despite suboptimality.
- Open access status: CHECK HANDBOOK - Handbook chapters may be partially available through Google Books. Likely behind publisher paywall.

**24. Anderson, J. R., & Pichert, J. W. (1978). Recall of previously unrecallable information following a shift in perspective.**
- Journal: *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 17*(1), 1-12.
- DOI: 10.1016/S0022-5371(78)90485-1
- Publisher: Elsevier (academic.elsevier.com)
- Why important: Shows schemas determine encoding and retrieval; information outside schema becomes inaccessible despite being present.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Elsevier. Check Princeton (Anderson's affiliation) for working papers or Google Scholar for PDF.

**25. Camerer, C. F., Loewenstein, G., & Weber, M. (1989). The curse of knowledge in economic settings: Experimental analyses of a phenomenon from economics, psychology, and the law.**
- Journal: *Journal of Political Economy, 97*(5), 1232-1254.
- DOI: 10.1086/261651
- Publisher: University of Chicago Press
- Why important: Introduces curse of knowledge mechanism; once you know something, you cannot imagine not knowing it; relevant to expert blindness.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Journal of Political Economy. Check Caltech (Camerer's affiliation) or author repositories for working papers.

**26. Hinds, P. J. (1999). The curse of expertise: The effects of expertise and debiasing methods on predictions of novice performance.**
- Journal: *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied, 5*(2), 205-221.
- DOI: 10.1037/1076-898X.5.2.205
- Publisher: American Psychological Association
- Why important: Extends curse of knowledge to expertise; experts worse than intermediates at predicting novice learning time.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - APA. Check Carnegie Mellon (Hinds's affiliation) for working papers.

**27. Clark, H. H. (2013). Whatever next? Predictive brains, situated agents, and the future of cognitive science.**
- Journal: *Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 36*(3), 181-204.
- DOI: 10.1017/S0140525X12000477
- Publisher: Cambridge University Press
- Why important: Predictive processing framework; strong priors suppress prediction errors to novel evidence; explains competency trap at neural level.
- Open access status: CHECK BBS - Some BBS papers have open commentaries. This major review may be partially available. Try publisher or author website.

**28. Hogarth, R. M., Lejarraga, T., & Soyer, E. (2015). Optimal experience of moderate familiarity facilitates choice.**
- Journal: *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Performance, 126*, 75-91.
- DOI: 10.1016/j.obhdp.2014.10.009
- Publisher: Elsevier
- Why important: Distinguishes kind versus wicked learning environments; affects whether competency becomes entrapping.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Elsevier. Check Barcelona GSE (Hogarth's affiliation) for working papers.

**29. Croskerry, P. (2002). Achieving quality in clinical decision making: Cognitive strategies and detection of bias.**
- Journal: *Academic Emergency Medicine, 9*(11), 1184-1204.
- DOI: 10.1111/j.1553-2712.2002.tb01574.x
- Publisher: Wiley
- Why important: Reviews diagnostic errors in medicine; premature closure as competency trap mechanism specific to expert pattern recognition.
- Open access status: CHECK WILEY - Some Academic Emergency Medicine articles may be open access. Check journal website or contact author.

## GROUP AND SOCIAL MECHANISMS

**30. Clark, H. H., & Wilkes-Gibbs, D. (1986). Referring as a collaborative process.**
- Journal: *Cognition, 22*(1), 1-39.
- DOI: 10.1016/0010-0277(86)90010-7
- Publisher: Elsevier
- Why important: FOUNDATIONAL PARADIGM - Reference games establishing conceptual pacts and convention formation. Basis for your image-labeling paradigm.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Elsevier. Clark is at Stanford; check Stanford repository or Google Scholar. Author likely has PDF available.

**31. Brennan, S. E., & Clark, H. H. (1996). Conceptual pacts and lexical choice in conversation.**
- Journal: *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 22*(6), 1482-1493.
- DOI: 10.1037/0278-7393.22.6.1482
- Publisher: American Psychological Association
- Why important: Shows conceptual pacts are partner-specific; new partners break established pacts. Directly models environmental shift in your paradigm.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - APA. Check Stanford repository or contact Clark (likely has PDF).

**32. Garrod, S., & Doherty, G. (1994). Conversation, co-ordination and convention: An experimental investigation of how groups establish linguistic conventions.**
- Journal: *Cognition, 53*(3), 181-215.
- DOI: 10.1016/0010-0277(94)90047-7
- Publisher: Elsevier
- Why important: Shows within-group interaction → strong conventions; cross-group interaction → flexible conventions. Convention formation at group level.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Elsevier. Check University of Glasgow (Garrod's affiliation) for repository version.

**33. Lewis, D. K. (1969). Convention: A philosophical study.**
- Publisher: Harvard University Press
- Why important: PHILOSOPHICAL FOUNDATION - Defines conventions game-theoretically; explains self-perpetuating nature of conventions.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - Classic book. Check university library or philosophy repository. Lewis's work may have online philosophy database availability.

**34. Young, H. P. (1993). The evolution of conventions.**
- Journal: *Econometrica, 61*(1), 57-84.
- DOI: 10.2307/2951778
- Publisher: The Econometric Society
- Why important: Formal model proving conventions are stochastically stable; success entrenches conventions mathematically. Competency trap is theoretically inevitable.
- Open access status: CHECK JSTOR - Econometrica is on JSTOR. Available through institutional subscription. Young may have working papers at Johns Hopkins.

**35. Van Huyck, J. B., Battalio, R. C., & Beil, R. O. (1990). Tacit coordination games, strategic uncertainty, and coordination failure.**
- Journal: *American Economic Review, 80*(1), 234-248.
- DOI: 10.2307/2006968
- Publisher: American Economic Association
- Why important: Lab experiment showing groups get trapped in Pareto-inferior equilibria; coordination failure mechanism for group-level competency traps.
- Open access status: CHECK AER - Some AER articles are open access on AEA website. Check journal or JSTOR. Authors' institutions may have working papers.

**36. Centola, D., & Baronchelli, A. (2015). The spontaneous emergence of conventions: An experimental study of cultural evolution.**
- Journal: *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 112*(7), 1989-1994.
- DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1418838112
- Publisher: National Academy of Sciences
- Why important: Online experiment showing convention emergence and stability; once emerged, conventions crowd out alternatives even if equally efficient.
- Open access status: OPEN ACCESS - PNAS publishes open access versions. Likely freely available on pnas.org.

**37. Stasser, G., & Titus, W. (1985). Pooling of unshared information in group decision making: Biased information sampling during discussion.**
- Journal: *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 48*(6), 1467-1478.
- DOI: 10.1037/0022-3514.48.6.1467
- Publisher: American Psychological Association
- Why important: Groups fail to pool unshared information; signals of environmental change held by individuals don't reach group discussions.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - APA. Check Miami University (Stasser's affiliation) or ResearchGate for author PDF.

**38. Janis, I. L. (1982). Groupthink: Psychological studies of policy decisions and fiascoes (2nd ed.).**
- Publisher: Houghton Mifflin
- Why important: Seminal work on groupthink; explains how cohesion and stress suppress dissent; prevents realistic appraisal of alternatives.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - Book. Check Google Books for preview/full access. University library should have.

**39. Wegner, D. M. (1987). Transactive memory: A contemporary analysis of the group mind.**
- In: Review of Personality and Social Psychology, 9*, 185-208.
- Publisher: Sage Publications
- Why important: Division of cognitive labor in groups; efficient but brittle when environment shifts. Groups lack expertise in new domains.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - Book chapter. Check Google Books or Sage online. Check University of Virginia (Wegner's affiliation) repository.

**40. Pickering, M. J., & Garrod, S. C. (2004). Toward a mechanistic psychology of dialogue.**
- Journal: *Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 27*(2), 169-226.
- DOI: 10.1017/S0140525X04000056
- Publisher: Cambridge University Press
- Why important: Interactive alignment model; automatic alignment at multiple levels forms shared mental models; alignment is implicit and hard to disrupt.
- Open access status: CHECK BBS - Cambridge BBS articles often have open versions. Try journal website; commentaries may be openly available.

## METHODOLOGICAL PRECEDENTS

**41. Brehmer, B. (1992). Dynamic decision making: Human performance in complex dynamic task environments.**
- In: G. A. Klein, J. Orasanu, R. Calderwood, & C. E. Zsambok (Eds.), *Decision making in action: Models and applications* (pp. 86-102). Ablex Publishing.
- Why important: Microworld paradigm; environment can change during experiment. Establishes methodology for measuring rigidity following environmental change.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Book chapter. Check Ablex archives or institutional library.

**42. Dörner, D. (1996). The logic of failure: Recognizing and avoiding error in complex situations.**
- Publisher: Basic Books
- Why important: Complex dynamic simulations reveal cognitive limitations. Competent-seeming strategies fail when environment changes.
- Open access status: BOOK - Published trade book. Likely paywalled. Check university library or purchase. Also published in German originally.

**43. Staw, B. M., Sandelands, L. E., & Dutton, J. E. (1981). Threat-rigidity effects in organizational behavior: A multilevel analysis.**
- Journal: *Administrative Science Quarterly, 26*(4), 501-524.
- DOI: 10.2307/2392337
- Publisher: JSTOR (Cornell University Press)
- Why important: Reviews evidence for threat rigidity as alternative to competency trap mechanism. Establishes need to distinguish threat from success effects.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - ASQ behind JSTOR. Check authors' institutional repositories (Northwestern, Stanford).

**44. Meiran, N. (1996). Reconfiguration of processing mode prior to task performance.**
- Journal: *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 22*(6), 1423-1442.
- DOI: 10.1037/0278-7393.22.6.1423
- Publisher: American Psychological Association
- Why important: Task-switching paradigm measuring switching costs. Precedent for measuring cost of strategy switching.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - APA. Check Ben-Gurion University (Meiran's affiliation) for working papers or Google Scholar.

**45. Monsell, S. (2003). Task switching.**
- Journal: *Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 7*(3), 134-140.
- DOI: 10.1016/S1364-6613(03)00028-7
- Publisher: Elsevier
- Why important: Review of task-switching literature. Establishes theoretical framework for understanding switching costs.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Elsevier. Check University of Exeter (Monsell's affiliation) or ResearchGate for author PDF.

**46. Knoblich, G., Ohlsson, S., Haider, H., & Rhenius, D. (1999). Constraint relaxation and chunk decomposition in insight problem solving.**
- Journal: *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 25*(6), 1534-1555.
- DOI: 10.1037/0278-7393.25.6.1534
- Publisher: American Psychological Association
- Why important: Insight problem paradigm allowing control of activated representations. Precedent for manipulating entrenchment experimentally.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - APA. Check University of Göttingen (Knoblich's affiliation) for author papers.

**47. Hills, T. T., Todd, P. M., Lazer, D., Redish, A. D., & Couzin, I. D. (2015). Exploration versus exploitation in space, mind, and society.**
- Journal: *Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 19*(1), 46-54.
- DOI: 10.1016/j.tics.2014.10.004
- Publisher: Elsevier
- Why important: Review of exploration-exploitation tradeoff across levels. Shows environmental volatility interacts with competence to determine exploration rates.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Elsevier. Check Indiana University (Hills's affiliation) for repository version.

**48. Lazaridou, A., Peysakhovich, A., & Baroni, M. (2017). Multi-agent cooperation and the emergence of natural language.**
- Conference: International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR)
- DOI: Not typical; check ICLR proceedings
- Why important: Computational analog of reference games; shows emergent communication conventions fail to generalize. Computational support for paradigm.
- Open access status: LIKELY OPEN - ICLR often has open proceedings. Check openreview.net or author repositories (Facebook AI, Tsinghua, Universitat Autonoma).

## ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS LITERATURE

**49. Hannan, M. T., & Freeman, J. H. (1977). The population ecology of organizations.**
- Journal: *American Journal of Sociology, 82*(5), 929-964.
- DOI: 10.1086/226424
- Publisher: JSTOR (University of Chicago Press)
- Why important: Selection argument; inertia selected for by environment rather than caused by mechanism. Major alternative to cognitive accounts.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - AJS behind JSTOR. Check Stanford or UC Berkeley (authors' affiliations) for working papers.

**50. Arkes, H. R., & Blumer, C. (1985). The psychology of sunk cost.**
- Journal: *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 35*(1), 124-140.
- DOI: 10.1016/0749-5978(85)90049-4
- Publisher: Elsevier
- Why important: Sunk cost fallacy as alternative mechanism. People persist due to prior investment independent of competence.
- Open access status: PAYWALLED - Elsevier. Check Ohio University (Arkes's affiliation) or ResearchGate.

**51. Nickerson, R. S. (1998). Confirmation bias: A ubiquitous phenomenon in many guises.**
- Journal: *Review of General Psychology, 2*(2), 175-220.
- DOI: 10.1037/1089-2680.2.2.175
- Publisher: American Psychological Association
- Why important: Comprehensive review of confirmation bias as universal tendency; questions whether expertise-specific entrenchment is needed as explanation.
- Open access status: LIKELY OPEN - American Psychological Association reviews sometimes open. Check APA website or author (Tufts University).

**52. Ocasio, W. (1997). Towards an attention-based view of the firm.**
- Journal: *Strategic Management Journal, 18*(S1), 187-206.
- DOI: 10.1002/(SICI)1097-0266(199707)18:1+<187::AID-SMJ936>3.0.CO;2-Y
- Publisher: Wiley
- Why important: Attention allocation framework; change not recognized if doesn't capture attention. Attention mediation alternative.
- Open access status: LIKELY PAYWALLED - Wiley. Check Northwestern (Ocasio's affiliation) or Google Scholar for PDF.

## PRIORITIZATION FOR CAMPUS ACCESS VISITS

### Must Obtain (Foundational/Critical):
- Audia et al. (2000) - Only prior controlled lab competency trap experiment
- Bilalic et al. (2008a, 2008b) - Only attentional mechanism studies with experts
- Henderson & Clark (1990) - Architectural knowledge distinction
- Clark & Wilkes-Gibbs (1986) - Paradigm foundation for your work
- Tripsas & Gavetti (2000) - Mechanism-focused case study
- Levinthal & March (1993) - Three forms of myopia framework

### Should Obtain (Important but secondary):
- March (1991) - Theoretical foundation
- Levitt & March (1988) - Definition of competency trap
- Leonard-Barton (1992) - Capabilities as rigidities
- Tushman & Anderson (1986) - Type of environmental change matters
- Dane (2010) - Cognitive entrenchment theory

### Nice to Have (If time):
- Miller (1994) - Icarus Paradox cases
- Sull (1999) - Active inertia concept
- Teece et al. (1997) - Dynamic capabilities
- Young (1993) - Mathematical proof of convention stability
- Van Huyck et al. (1990) - Coordination failure experiments
