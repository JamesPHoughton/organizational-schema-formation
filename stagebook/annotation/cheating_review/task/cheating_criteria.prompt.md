## What counts as cheating

The group's task was to coordinate on labels purely through the on-camera video conversation, and then to recall those labels silently and independently. Cheating means using an outside channel to remember or share labels.

We only have video from the **Discussion** stages — not the Recall stages. So your job is to look for evidence, during Discussion, that the participants are setting up a way to share or recall labels outside the platform. The two most prominent failure modes are:

- **Taking screenshots or photos of the image panel** — participant brings a phone up to the screen, switches windows to a screenshot tool, or appears to be capturing the panel.
- **Off-platform communication** — visible signs of a second channel: typing into another app/window, looking at or holding a phone for sustained periods, talking with someone off-camera, an extra device nearby, audio cues that suggest a parallel call.

Other things to flag if you see them:

- **Writing labels down on paper or in a text file** — useful to the participant only if they can reference it later, but worth flagging when clearly visible.
- **A third person in the room or audibly prompting** the participant.

Brief glances away from the screen, normal pauses to think, fidgeting with a phone briefly, and expressive gestures during discussion are **not** cheating. Cheating requires evidence that the participant is moving information into or out of a channel the study can't see.

Recall happens in a separate stage where the participants cannot see or hear each other, so talking *during* recall is not a concern — but anything during Discussion that sets up sharing or referencing labels later is.

When you are unsure, prefer **Possible cheating** over Clear cheating, and use the notes box to describe what you saw and at roughly what time.
